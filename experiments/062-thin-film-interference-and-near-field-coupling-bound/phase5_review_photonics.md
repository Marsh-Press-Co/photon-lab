## 1. Does EM-2/EM-3/EM-4's resolution actually close my own Phase-2 concern?

My own (this program's, not necessarily my continuity, but the seat's) Phase-2 critique flagged that EM-3's broadband/narrowband "resonance discriminator" was a claims-language proxy, not a physical test, and — worse — could point backwards: an angle-*integrated* (hemispherical/integrating-sphere) OD measurement can smear a genuinely narrowband, critically-coupled resonant dip into an apparently broadband reading, since the round-trip interference phase `2β=2(2π/λ)n₂d·cosθ_t` is angle-dependent. My flip demanded a measurement-geometry query and an amended falsification condition. Red Team adopted this in full as mandatory fix 2; Phase 3 added query 14 and amended `NOTES.md` §8 accordingly.

**What Phase 4 actually delivered is not the specular-vs-integrated disclosure I asked for — it is a different, more fundamental finding that supersedes the question rather than answering it directly.** Query 14 (corroborated by queries 2, 3, 6) establishes that the LCD-class black-matrix OD convention is measured in **transmission** through a **transparent, unbacked substrate** — no reflective backing is present in the measurement geometry at all. Since a Salisbury-screen/critically-coupled resonant absorber's entire mechanism *requires* a reflective backing to interfere against, the mechanism is **structurally inapplicable** regardless of whether the measurement angle is specular or angle-integrated. If there is no back reflector, the angle-dependence of a resonance condition that doesn't exist is moot. In that narrow logical sense, this **does** close my concern — it doesn't answer "was this angle-averaged," it makes the answer irrelevant.

I verified this is not merely asserted: `phase1_proposal.md` §4.1's own model (an Airy stack between air, an absorbing layer, and a substrate `n₃`) is a strictly *backed* model; if `n₃` is just glass (transparent, non-reflective) rather than a metal/reflective layer, `r₂₃` is small and non-resonant by construction, and the whole Salisbury-screen alternative in §4.4 never gets traction. That is genuine physics, not spin.

**But this resolution is weaker than it reads, and I flag it, not clear it, for one reason the document does not address.** The chain is: query 2 (a *generic* LCD-black-matrix-industry OD convention, not shown to be this specific Brewer Science patent's own measurement protocol) + query 14 (the patent's 1998–99 date and its own "TFT or STN displays" target language, textually classified as pre-OLED/LCD-class) ⇒ *this patent's own OD figure* was measured in transmission on an unbacked substrate. That is an inference by product-category classification, not a direct read of the patent's own methodology section — T18 still blocks reading it directly. More concretely, **query 1's own finding is never reconciled**: "found spectrophotometric transmittance-**AND**-reflectance characterization methodology in general use" for this exact Brewer-Science-class material. If reflectance characterization is *also* in general use for this material class, the categorical "LCD-class ⇒ transmission-only, no backing" argument needs to explain why that doesn't apply here, and `phase4_results.md` does not attempt it. The finding is directionally sound, well-reasoned, and — if it holds — a *stronger* answer than the one I asked for. It is not yet a primary-source-verified fact about the one patent MP-3 actually cites, and the document should say so rather than stating "structurally inapplicable" without qualification. **Recommendation for Phase 5's mandatory-fix docket**: add one sentence to EM-3's Phase-4 verdict acknowledging the inferential (product-category) basis of the "unbacked substrate" conclusion and naming query 1's own unreconciled transmittance-AND-reflectance finding as the residual uncertainty — this is cheap, and it is exactly the kind of caveat-completeness gap this program's own house discipline exists to catch before a future cycle cites "structurally inapplicable" as settled fact.

EM-2 and EM-4 ride on the same resolution and are correctly scored given it: transmission-based (no ÷2 correction) is independently corroborated by two separate query hits (2 and 6) using different phrasing, which is real, not circular — I treat EM-2 as solidly CONFIRMED. EM-4's arithmetic (ratio stands at exactly 1.20×) is trivial given EM-2/EM-3 and correct.

**A further, un-flagged internal-consistency gap, adjacent to but outside the formal six-row table: EM-6/EM-7's OD→α conversion silently drops Item A's own R-vs-T distinction.** Item A (§4.2, EM-2) established that a *reflectance*-based OD implies a **round-trip** through the absorbing layer, so the correctly-inferred single-pass α needs **half** the τ a transmission-based OD would give (`τ_R = τ_T/2`). (A further complication, noted but not scored: for a several-mm-thick, highly porous aerogel monolith, neither the T-based nor R-based *planar Airy-stack* limit is obviously the right model at all — a diffuse-scattering/radiative-transfer treatment would be more appropriate than either point-optics limit this document borrows from Item A.
